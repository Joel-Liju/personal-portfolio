## Ingredients
**For the Chicken and Marinade**
- 1 1/2 lbs bone-in, skinless chicken thighs or drumsticks
- 1/2 cup plain yogurt (not sour)
- 1 1/2 tablespoons ginger-garlic paste
- 1/2 tablespoon garam masala or biryani masala
- 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1/2 teaspoon red chili powder
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

**For the Par-Boiled Rice**
- 1 1/2 cups premium aged basmati rice
- 3 quarts water
- 3/4 cup salt (for boiling water)
- 1 bay leaf
- 4 green cardamoms
- 6 whole cloves
- 1 inch cinnamon stick
- 1 star anise
- 3/4 teaspoon shahi jeera (caraway seeds)

**For the Assembly and Layering**
- 1/4 cup ghee or neutral oil
- 2 medium red onions, thinly sliced
- 1/4 cup chopped walnuts, almonds, or pine nuts
- 1 pinch saffron threads steeped in 3 tablespoons warm milk
- 1/4 cup fresh mint leaves, chopped
- 1/4 cup fresh cilantro leaves, chopped

## Directions
**Marinade and Prep**
1. Mix the yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, garam masala, turmeric, red chili powder, lemon juice, and salt in a large bowl. 
2. Make deep slits into the chicken pieces, add them to the bowl, and coat thoroughly. Cover and refrigerate for at least 8 hours, or overnight.
3. Rinse the basmati rice in cold water at least three times until the water runs clear, then soak it in fresh water for 30 minutes. Drain completely.

**Rice and Crispy Onions**
1. Heat the ghee or oil in a heavy-bottomed Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and fry, stirring often, until deep golden brown and crispy. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels.
2. In the same fat, fry the chopped nuts for 3 to 4 minutes until toasted and golden. Set aside.
3. Bring 3 quarts of water to a boil in a large pot with the 3/4 cup of salt and all the whole spices (bay leaf, cardamoms, cloves, cinnamon, star anise, shahi jeera). 
4. Add the drained rice to the boiling water and cook for exactly 4 minutes until al dente (halfway cooked). Drain immediately.

**Layering and Dum Cooking**
1. Arrange the marinated chicken and its marinade in a single flat layer at the bottom of the Dutch oven.
2. Scatter half of the fried onions, toasted nuts, fresh mint, and fresh cilantro over the chicken.
3. Layer the par-boiled rice evenly over the top of the herbs and chicken.
4. Drizzle the saffron milk mixture and any remaining melted ghee over the rice layer. Top with the remaining fried onions and nuts.
5. Seal the pot tightly by placing a piece of aluminum foil over the top before pressing the lid down to trap the steam. 
6. Place over low heat and cook (the "dum" method) for 45 minutes. Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, unopened, for an additional 30 minutes before mixing and serving with raita.

## What I liked

I like how saffron, cardamom, and slow-steamed rice make the whole dish feel special; every layer brings warmth without losing the rice's character.

## Notes
- **The Rice Importance**: Sohla emphasizes that "biryani is all about the rice." Using a high-quality, long-grain premium aged basmati rice is essential for non-sticky, distinct grains.
- **The Salt Secret**: The 3/4 cup of salt in the rice water looks alarming, but it acts like pasta water—the rice only absorbs a fraction of it during its short 4-minute boil, seasoning the grains perfectly from the inside out.
- **Dum Separation**: If you do not have a thick, heavy cast-iron Dutch oven, place a flat iron griddle (or tava) directly on the stove burner and set your pot on top of it. This diffuses the heat and prevents the chicken layer at the bottom from scorching during the long steam.
