## Ingredients
- 8 ounces freshly ground beef chuck (80/20 blend), divided into four 2-ounce balls
- 2 high-quality hamburger buns (such as Martin's Potato Rolls)
- 2 slices American cheese
- 1 tablespoon unsalted butter, softened
- Kosher salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste
- Condiments as desired (such as thinly sliced onions, pickles, ketchup, mustard, or burger sauce)

## Directions
1. **Prepare the Buns and Skillet**: Spread the softened butter evenly over the cut sides of the burger buns. Heat a large, heavy cast iron skillet or flat stainless steel griddle over medium-high heat until it is smoking hot. Place the buns cut-side down in the pan and toast for 1 to 2 minutes until deep golden brown. Remove the buns and set aside.
2. **Smash the Beef**: Increase the heat under the skillet to high. Place two of the 2-ounce beef balls into the smoking hot pan, leaving plenty of space between them. Immediately place a square of parchment paper over a beef ball, and press down vertically with a heavy, flat spatula (using a second tool like a wooden spoon to apply extra leverage) until the patty is completely flat and ultra-thin—under 1/4-inch thick. Repeat with the second ball. Remove the parchment paper.
3. **Season and Sear**: Season the smashed patties generously with kosher salt and black pepper. Allow them to cook undisturbed for roughly 2 minutes, until the outer edges are deeply browned, lacy, and crisp, and juices begin to pool on top.
4. **Scrape, Flip, and Melt**: Using a sturdy, sharp-edged metal spatula held at a shallow angle, aggressively scrape under the patty to ensure all the browned crust remains intact as you flip it over. Immediately place a slice of American cheese on top of one patty, then stack the second patty directly onto the cheesy layer. Cook for just 15 to 30 seconds longer to melt the cheese through.
5. **Assemble**: Transfer the stacked patties onto the bottom bun. Top with your preferred condiments (like pickles or onions) and close with the top toasted bun. Repeat the smashing and cooking process for the remaining two beef balls to make the second burger. Serve immediately.

## Notes
- **Crust Physics**: J. Kenji López-Alt emphasizes that you must smash the beef immediately within the first 30 seconds of hitting the hot pan. If you wait any longer, the fat will render out, the proteins will begin to set, and smashing later will squeeze out all the internal juices, leaving you with a dry burger. Smashing instantly welds the meat structure to the metal, maximizing the Maillard browning reaction.
- **The Right Tool**: You absolutely need a sharp, rigid metal spatula for this technique. A flexible fish spatula or plastic turner will not give you enough leverage to flatten the beef or the clean scraping power needed to lift that delicate, lacy crust off the cast iron without tearing it apart.
- **Meat Grind Matters**: Do not use lean ground beef. An 80/20 fat-to-lean ratio (ideally fresh-ground chuck) is critical. The rendering fat lubricates the meat and fries the edges of the patty in the pan, creating those iconic crispy, lacy fringes.
