## Ingredients
**Hollandaise Sauce**
- 4 egg yolks
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted
- 1 pinch cayenne pepper
- 1 pinch salt

**Eggs Benedict Assembly**
- 8 slices Canadian bacon
- 1 tablespoon butter
- 4 English muffins, split
- 1 tablespoon white vinegar
- 8 eggs
- Salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste

## Directions
**Hollandaise Sauce**
1. Whisk the egg yolks and lemon juice together in a stainless steel bowl until the mixture is thick and pale.
2. Place the bowl over a saucepan containing barely simmering water (the water should not touch the bottom of the bowl) to create a double boiler. Whisk the mixture rapidly, ensuring it doesn't get too hot or scramble.
3. Slowly drizzle in the melted butter a few drops at a time while whisking constantly until the sauce is thick and doubled in volume.
4. Remove the bowl from the heat, stir in the salt and cayenne pepper, and cover tightly to keep warm.

**Eggs Benedict Assembly**
1. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the Canadian bacon and cook for 1 to 2 minutes per side until heated through and lightly browned. Remove and set aside.
2. Lightly toast the split English muffins and spread them with the 1 tablespoon of butter. Place two muffin halves onto four separate serving plates and top each half with a slice of the warm Canadian bacon.
3. Fill a large saucepan with roughly 3 inches of water, add the white vinegar, and bring it to a gentle simmer. 
4. Crack an egg into a small cup, gently slide it into the simmering water, and repeat for the remaining eggs. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the egg whites are completely set but the yolks remain soft. Remove the poached eggs with a slotted spoon and drain them on a paper towel.
5. Place one poached egg on top of each Canadian bacon-topped English muffin half.
6. Ladle the warm hollandaise sauce generously over the top of each egg, season with salt and black pepper to taste, and serve immediately.

## Notes
- **Hollandaise Rescue**: If your hollandaise sauce begins to break or separate while whisking, take it completely off the heat and vigorously whisk in a single teaspoon of warm water until it smooths back out.
- **Poaching Trick**: Adding white vinegar to the poaching water is a professional trick that lowers the water's pH, helping the egg white proteins coagulate much faster so they wrap neatly around the yolk instead of feathering out into white ribbons.
- **Temperature Control**: Keep the water for the sauce at a bare simmer—if the steam gets too hot, the eggs will scramble instantly and create a chunky texture rather than a silky smooth emulsion.
